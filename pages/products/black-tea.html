About Vietnamese Black Tea

Vietnamese black tea is produced from the same tea plant as green tea, but is fully oxidized to develop its distinctive dark color and rich flavor. Grown in the cool highland regions where morning mist and afternoon sun create ideal conditions, Vietnamese black tea develops a naturally sweet, full-bodied taste with subtle floral notes. The production process involves careful oxidation that brings out deeper flavors while maintaining the tea's natural sweetness.

Black tea produced in Vietnam has gained recognition from tea enthusiasts worldwide for its balanced flavor - robust enough for everyday drinking, yet sophisticated enough for special occasions. Our black tea is made from whole leaves, preserving the complexity of flavor that broken leaves cannot deliver.

Characteristics & Flavor Profile

  • Appearance: Dark brown to black leaves with golden tips, indicating quality and proper oxidation
  • Aroma: Rich, warm aroma with subtle floral and fruity undertones
  • Flavor: Full-bodied, naturally sweet, with slight honey notes and smooth finish
  • Infusion: Deep amber to reddish-brown color, never harsh or overly astringent
  • Leaf Grade: Whole leaves ensure superior flavor and multiple resteepings

How to Brew Vietnamese Black Tea

  • Water Temperature: 95-100°C (boiling water is fine for black tea)
  • Tea-to-Water Ratio: 1 teaspoon per cup
  • Steeping Time: 3-4 minutes for first infusion, leaves can be resteeped 2-3 times
  • Best Practice: Whole leaf black tea holds its flavor through multiple infusions better than broken leaves
  • Pairing: Excellent with breakfast, afternoon pastries, or as an evening drink

The Oxidation Process

What makes black tea different from green tea is the oxidation process. After harvesting and initial withering, the tea leaves are rolled and spread to oxidize. This oxidation triggers chemical reactions that develop the darker color, richer flavors, and fuller body that characterize black tea. Vietnamese black tea is fully oxidized (90-100%) to develop its characteristic bold flavor while maintaining natural sweetness that makes it approachable for all tea drinkers.

Versatility & Uses

  • Everyday Tea: Full-bodied enough to enjoy plain or with breakfast
  • With Milk or Sugar: Black tea's strength pairs well with traditional additions
  • Iced Tea: Makes excellent cold brew tea for hot weather
  • Tea Blends: Popular base for chai and flavored tea blends
  • Multiple Infusions: Whole leaves can be steeped multiple times with continued flavor

Storage & Shelf Life

Black tea is more robust than green tea and stores well for extended periods. Keep in a cool, dry place in airtight containers away from strong odors. Black tea properly stored in sealed containers can maintain quality for 1-2 years or longer. Unlike green tea, black tea actually develops deeper, more complex flavors as it ages, similar to wine. This makes Vietnamese black tea a good choice for long-term storage if properly sealed.
